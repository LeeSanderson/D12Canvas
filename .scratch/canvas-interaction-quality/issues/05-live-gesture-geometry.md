# Live gesture geometry

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 01

## Question

Decide how geometry that is *in flight* during a gesture reaches everything that depends on it, before the gesture commits.

Two of the seed notes — connectors not staying attached through a resize, and connectors updating only after a shape stops moving — are the same defect. `ComponentContainer` mutates its own local `X`/`Y`/`Width`/`Height` during a drag or resize and only raises `OnMoved`/`OnResized` on mouseup. `DiagramCanvas` computes every edge's endpoints from `ComponentInstance.Bounds` on the `Board`, which does not change until that commit. So the container moves and the edges do not. The move branch in `HandleMouseMove` does not even call `NotifyStateChanged`, so nothing outside the container re-renders at all.

Decide:

- Where in-flight geometry lives. Options include a transient overlay on `Board` reads, a gesture-scoped store owned by the arbitration model from ticket 01, or the container continuing to own it with a push channel outward. ADR 0003 is settled, so `Board`'s persisted shape cannot change — but whether *reads* can be intercepted is open.
- What else needs live geometry besides edges. The selection-anchored property bar (08) must track a moving selection; alignment guides (11) are meaningless without it; multi-selection bounding boxes and group bounds are computed from members.
- How this stays consistent with the gesture-level history model in ADR 0007. A gesture is exactly one history entry; live geometry must not produce intermediate commands, and must roll back cleanly if the gesture is cancelled or leaks (ticket 04).
- The render-cost question. `d12canvas-next` tickets 01/02 found that Blazor re-rendering every mounted child regardless of its own state was the real bottleneck, fixed with skip-if-unchanged plus a throttled pan `StateHasChanged`. Live edge updates reintroduce per-mousemove pressure on a different path — decide whether the same throttling applies, whether edges need their own render path, and whether this needs a stated budget or falls out of the design.
- Whether the LOD placeholder path (ADR 0011) and windowed mounting need to participate, or can ignore in-flight geometry entirely.

## Answer

Recorded as **ADR 0020**. Two `CONTEXT.md` terms added (`Gesture preview`, `Live geometry`), one widened (`Bounds`).

### The mechanism was already half-built, and the ticket's first bullet is narrower than it reads

`EffectiveBounds` (`DiagramCanvas.razor.cs:2117`) already *is* the "transient overlay on `Board` reads" option, and it already works: it returns an instance's bounds offset by the live group-move delta or scaled within the live group-resize bbox, and it is already read by `OrderedTabStops()` — which is what feeds `ComponentContainer`'s `X`/`Y`/`Width`/`Height`, the LOD threshold test and tab order — and by `SelectedInstancesBounds()`. So for group move and group resize, containers, the selection bbox and tab stops **already track live**.

What does not read it: `Board.ResolveEndpoint` (`Board.cs:36`), which is where edges, edge labels and floating-endpoint markers come from; `Board.GetBounds(Group)`, so a group's own tab stop stays behind while its members track (`:1763`); `Board.GetVisible`; `Board.FindPortNear`; and `UpdateMarqueeSelection`.

And the single-instance path publishes nothing at all — `ComponentContainer.HandleMouseMove` mutates its own `X`/`Y` *parameters* and the canvas learns nothing until `OnMoved` at release. **ADR 0018 already deletes that**: `_isMoving`/`_isGroupMoving` collapse into one canvas-owned mover, so the single-instance case arrives on the group path and inherits whatever this ticket decides. The question was therefore never "invent a mechanism" — it was whether the existing overlay becomes *the* seam and whether every consumer routes through it.

### What was decided

**A published map, not a derived function.** The preview is written by exactly one thing and read by everything. `EffectiveBounds` is literally `if (_isGroupMoving) … if (_isGroupResizing) …` — a switch over gesture kinds inside a geometry reader, which is what ADR 0018 refused for the pointer surface and ADR 0017 for hit precedence, and this ticket multiplies the readers. Publishing puts each gesture's arithmetic in its own file and leaves readers with no branch. An overlay `Board` was rejected for wrapping a gesture-aware decorator around the persisted model and hiding live-versus-committed at every call site, the serializer's included.

**Two typed slots, one read surface.** Walking the eight gestures leaves exactly two shapes: bounds overrides (`MoveSelection`, `ResizeSelection`) and a single point (`DragEdgeEnd`). So the preview carries bounds overrides keyed by instance id, plus at most one **pending edge line** — two absolute board points and the id of the edge whose own line is suppressed, null when drawing a new edge. One by construction: one pointer, one gesture, one dragged end. The second slot exists so edge geometry has *one* live read path, folding `IsBeingEdited`/`ConnectPreviewLine` into the same lookup that makes an attached edge follow a moving instance. The cost of not having it is already on record: the edge *line* was taught about the connector drag, then the edge *label* had to be taught separately (`:1552`), and 08, 11 and 19 are all new readers.

**Encoding the pending line as a `Bounds` was tried and does not work** — a rect spanning two points cannot say which corner is which (the diagonals collapse), and `SourceArrow`/`TargetArrow` mean losing the orientation draws the arrowhead on the wrong end; a signed extent to recover it poisons `Bounds` for `Right`/`Bottom`/`Intersects`/`Union`/`ExpandedBy`/`PointAtFraction`, which all assume non-negative sides (the marquee already normalises with `Math.Abs` for that reason); an `Edge` has no `Bounds` at all, so the entry would *mean* something different from an instance's and force readers to branch on entity kind — two places to ask, disguised as one; and a brand-new edge has no id to key on. The uniformity that idea wanted lives one level up instead: **no caller touches the slots.**

**Derived geometry leaves the model, and each derivation gets two named entry points.** Threading an optional bounds source through `Board`'s four geometry methods was rejected on its failure mode — every call site must remember, and forgetting silently reverts that one site to today's defect with no compile error. Instead each derivation exists once, with a committed entry point on `Board` (public, what a host means) and a live one on `LiveGeometry` (internal, what the rendering path calls); shared logic like a group's recursive member walk is parameterised by its bounds source, but the *choice* of source is never shared, because that is what a call site should be forced to state. The rule: **read `instance.Bounds` for committed state — a command's before-value, persistence — and go through live geometry for what is on screen now.** Nothing prevents a raw read; the rule is what makes one legible rather than a trap. The library calls a committed entry in exactly two places, both deliberate and both named (windowing, content extent), stated rather than claiming the rendering path is purely live.

The consequence worth banking, because getting it backwards inverts a class of commits: because `Board` is never written mid-gesture, `instance.Bounds` at release still holds the press-time value, so a command's `before` comes straight off the field and needs no snapshot. Reading `before` through live geometry would compare the preview against itself and record a no-op.

**The preview is the truth.** The commit writes the last published preview verbatim rather than recomputing. Today's commit recomputes *and disagrees with what was shown*: `MoveComponent` applies `SnapBounds` at commit while the drag previewed unsnapped (`:1201`), so with snap-to-grid on a dragged instance tracks the cursor and then **jumps to the grid on release** — a felt defect not in the seed's ten. `CommitGroupMove` does not snap at all, so behaviour also differs by selection size, and ADR 0018's collapse of the two movers forces that divergence to be resolved here. So snapping moves into the tick: one snap per tick over the selection as a **rigid body**, no branch on n, following ADR 0014's finding that you snap the *target coordinate* rather than each result. Three payoffs — ticket 11 becomes possible at all (a guide that shows alignment while the commit lands elsewhere is a lie the user watches happen); the commit stops being a second implementation of the gesture's arithmetic, so the two cannot drift; and three scattered no-op guards collapse into one rule, *commit only entities whose previewed bounds differ from committed*, covering a release that never promoted, a drag that returns to its origin, and a snap that rounds back. This does **not** weaken ADR 0018's `final delta = release − press`: that identity is about the raw delta JS hands the gesture, and snapping is a transform applied after receiving an exact one.

**No pointer gesture creates a command before release** — elevated from convention to invariant, which is what makes revert *discarding a dictionary*. `pointercancel`, `Escape` and ADR 0018's loud `lostpointercapture` net all reduce to dropping the preview, and ADR 0007's one-entry-per-gesture stops being a discipline anyone can forget. It has teeth: it forbids intermediate commands coalesced at release (crash-safe live undo, bought with history surgery ADR 0007 rules out).

Scoped deliberately to *pointer* gestures. `NudgeCommand` creates its command on first keydown, writes `Bounds` immediately and grows in place via `Extend`, so a held arrow key is one entry with no preview — correct rather than an exception, because a keypress's result is fully determined the moment it is pressed while a pointer move stays provisional until release. **Ticket 16 should therefore not reach for the preview**; it would add provisionality where none exists.

**Cadence, and a structural budget.** Rate limiting moves into JS as **at most one `OnPointerMoved` per animation frame** — `pointermove` fires at device rate and outpaces frames several times over, and this is the same principle ADR 0018 applied to the threshold (JS owns the rate, C# receives meaningful events). The C#-side 16ms `DateTime.UtcNow` check goes away. What makes coalescing safe: **every gesture derives from the press-anchored absolute delta**, never an accumulated one — pan currently accumulates and resets its anchor per tick, so it must see every event or lose motion (its own comment at `:1990` says so); as `panOrigin + (current − press)` a dropped frame costs nothing, and ADR 0018 already supplies the press-anchored delta. **An edge becomes its own component** with `ShouldRender` on its resolved endpoints — the trick `ComponentContainer` already uses and the split ADR 0015 already made — because otherwise dragging two instances on a 500-edge board recomputes and diffs every edge every frame. Budget stated structurally rather than in milliseconds (device-dependent, untestable in CI): **one frame per pointer move, with per-frame work proportional to participants and the edges touching them, not board size** — the same shape as ADR 0015's "a pan costs one transform write". Side effect worth having: because the preview is a data structure rather than pixels, "does the edge follow the shape" is assertable without a browser, which is something ticket 15 can build on.

**Windowing reads committed; LOD freezes at press.** Opposite answers, different reasons. Windowing on committed bounds makes mounting *automatically* stable across a move or resize with no pin rule to write or forget, since committed bounds do not move — read live and an instance dragged past viewport+overscan vanishes from the user's hand (the gesture survives it, since ADR 0018 put capture on `.diagram-canvas` precisely so an unmount cannot break it, but you would be dragging nothing). This correctly leaves `Pan` alone: mounting depends on the viewport *and* committed bounds, and a pan changes the viewport, which is the point. LOD freezes for participants because `LodSizeThreshold` has no hysteresis, so holding a resize handle at the boundary would mount and unmount an author's whole component tree at frame rate — and even one crossing discards whatever internal state it holds (scroll position, an open dropdown, focus). Hysteresis stops the flapping but not that. This looks like an exception to preview-is-truth and is not: the preview governs *where* things are, not the fidelity they are drawn at, and because the commit writes the preview verbatim the state resolved at release is the right one for what was being manipulated. Cost is a small pop at release.

**ADR 0011 is confirmed, not amended, and the deviation was in the code.** It specifies the cutoff as "computed at render time from a component's own bounds and the current scale" — and `Bounds` is the committed field, so reading committed during a gesture is what it literally says. Feeding `EffectiveBounds` into `IsBelowLodThreshold` is where the code drifted from it. **Third time on this map** that reading an ADR rather than a summary of it changed the answer (ticket 14's ADR 0012, ticket 18's ADR 0011).

**The minimap and content extent stay committed on purpose.** ADR 0015 already put the minimap's boxes behind a `ShouldRender` keyed on **board revision**, and a preview is not a board revision — so committed reading is that design working as intended rather than anything this ticket changes. At minimap scale a 200-unit drag is sub-pixel, so per-frame invalidation of an uncapped box layer buys motion nobody can see. The split falls on a line already true: the **viewport rect stays live** because it reads `ZoomPanTracker`, not the preview, so a pan tracks exactly while content settles at release. Content extent follows, being the same shared computation, and framing mid-gesture would move the viewport out from under a live gesture. Recorded with its reason attached, because without one it reads as an oversight and someone will "fix" it.

### The one correction to the ticket's own framing

The ticket sends the transition question nowhere, but ADR 0019 already answered it: line 52 names the blanket `transition: transform 0.1s ease-out` as applying to "**every** transform write, including a pointer drag, where it is pure lag — the canvas does not track the pointer, it eases toward it. That blanket rule ends here." So suppressing easing while a pointer gesture is active is ADR 0019 being honoured, not a decision taken here.

### What this amends

**ADR 0018, in one place**: `OnPointerMoved` is rate-limited in JS to at most one call per animation frame. The four `[JSInvokable]` methods keep their shape and the threshold rule is untouched — declared rather than left to a reader, since 0018 promises a press that never promotes produces exactly two calls. **ADR 0007 confirmed and strengthened**, with its scope clarified in one respect (the invariant binds pointer gestures; the keyboard satisfies one-entry-per-gesture by writing through and extending in place). **ADR 0003 untouched** — no overlay, no wrapper, nothing added to the persisted model. **ADRs 0011, 0015 and 0019 confirmed**, per above. **ADR 0014 reused, not amended.**
