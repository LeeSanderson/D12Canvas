# 71 — Pointer arbitration spine

**What to build:** Every press on the canvas is classified once, in the browser, and exactly one `Pointer gesture` owns it until release (ADRs 0017 classification half, 0018, 0022, 0031, 0036, 0038, 0066, 0025). This ticket builds the whole spine and moves the two canvas-owned gestures onto it:

- A `pointerdown` listener on the canvas element walks up from the event target to the nearest marked element and hands C# a `Hit target` of `(role, entityId, part)` plus press count, `pointerType`, buttons and modifiers. All eleven roles are classified from the start; `canvas` means the walk found nothing.
- Five decisions are taken synchronously before the interop hop: `preventDefault`, `setPointerCapture` on a stable element the library controls, the single `Focus transfer` to the canvas element (which gains `tabindex="-1"` and is focused with `preventScroll`), the 4 screen-pixel `Drag threshold` (JavaScript never calls C# below it), and native-menu suppression. Moves are coalesced to one call per animation frame. The press point is converted to board space once, so a delta is always release minus press.
- Four public `[JSInvokable]` entry points (`OnPointerPressed`, `OnPointerMoved`, `OnPointerReleased`, `OnPointerCancelled`) and one reusable `addPointerListener` with `classify` on or off. The gesture kind is chosen in C# after the hop because it needs the selection. Gestures are objects over an explicit context (board, selection, `ZoomPanTracker`, commit path), never a switch, with phases `pointing`, `active`, `cancelled`.
- `Pan` and `MarqueeSelect` are built. A plain left-drag on empty canvas draws the marquee, Shift+drag adds the band's contents to the selection, a quick click on empty canvas clears the selection, the right and middle buttons pan whatever they land on, a middle click below the threshold does nothing, and a right-click release from `pointing` resolves the selection (preserve if inside it, select the pressed entity, clear on empty canvas) and opens the existing menu at the press point.
- Ownership is keyed by `pointerId` and the claiming button; other buttons' down and up are dropped. `pointerup` commits, `pointercancel` reverts, `lostpointercapture` on a live gesture reverts and writes `console.error`. A window `blur` cancels and ends the press. Escape cancels: drop the preview, restore the `Selection snapshot` taken at press, mark `cancelled`, keep capture until the claiming button comes up, and that release does nothing. The viewport is never restored.
- While any gesture owns the press, nothing writes `Board` except that gesture's release and no keyboard command changes the selection. The guard sits on `CommandHistory` and the two selection-only handlers; Escape, copy, snap toggle, focus moves and viewport commands stay live. A host replacing the `Board` reference cancels without restoring the snapshot.
- The canvas-level `@onmousedown`, `@onmousemove`, `@onmouseup`, `@onclick` and `@oncontextmenu` bindings are deleted, along with `_dragMoved`, `_isPanning`, `_isMarqueeSelecting` and the pan render throttle.

Until ticket 78 lands, a press whose role is still served by an old handler (`instance`, `resize-handle`, `port`, `port-strip`, `edge`, `edge-endpoint`, `edge-label`, `selection-bounds`, `selection-handle`, `author-content`) is left to that handler: the listener takes none of the synchronous decisions for it and C# chooses no gesture. Preventing `pointerdown` would suppress the compatibility mouse events those handlers rely on. Ticket 78 removes the allowance.

**Blocked by:** None — can start immediately

## Comments

The spine is a `D12Canvas.Pointer` namespace and one listener. `addPointerListener(canvas, container, dotnetRef, { classify })` in `DiagramCanvas.razor.js` walks from the event target to the nearest `data-d12-role` marker (an unmarked natively interactive element or a `data-d12-author-content` marker met first makes the press `author-content`), reads the entity id from the nearest marked ancestor so a resize handle or a port arrives naming the instance it belongs to, takes the five synchronous decisions, counts presses itself (pointer events carry no click count), coalesces moves to one `OnPointerMoved` per animation frame, forwards `pointerup` only for the claiming button after flushing any pending move, and turns `pointercancel`, `lostpointercapture` (with the `console.error`) and window `blur` into `OnPointerCancelled(reason)`. Coordinates cross as container-relative screen pixels, measured against the container's own rect at the event, so no round trip stands between a press and its owner. The canvas element carries `tabindex="-1"` and `@ref`, and `HandleCanvasFocus` on it drops the keyboard's tab-stop anchor and any port pick in progress. The native-menu decision needs a rule for a secondary press on author content, and the one shipped is ADR 0047's order (editable target, live text selection, link or media keep the browser's menu; everything else is the canvas's), ahead of the ticket that owns the `Menu verdict` marker, because the binding it replaces suppressed the browser's menu whenever anything was selected and a worse rule would have been a regression inside an inline text edit.

On the C# side `PointerPress`, `PointerMove` and `PointerRelease` are the public interop records; everything else is internal. `PressToKind.Resolve` maps a press to a `GestureKind` (secondary or middle on any role is `Pan`, primary on `canvas` is `MarqueeSelect`, primary on any other role is null until that role's gesture lands). `PointerGesture` is the abstract owner with `Pointing`, `Active` and `Cancelled` phases: a move promotes to active, a release from pointing runs the click outcome, a release from active commits, a cancelled gesture's release does nothing. `PanGesture` is press-anchored (`panAtPress + (move - press)`) and its secondary click resolves the selection by role then asks the context to open the menu at the press point. `MarqueeSelectGesture` converts the anchor to board space once, replaces the selection every tick with what the band intersects (unioned with the press-time selection when Shift was held at press), and clears the selection on a click. Both see the canvas only through `IGestureContext`, and `CanvasGestureContext` is the one adapter, which opens the menu only when the selection is eligible for one.

Cancellation is canvas-level and three steps, in `CancelActiveGesture`: drop the marquee band, restore the `SelectionSnapshot` taken at press, mark the gesture cancelled. Escape's first rung does exactly that and returns; a second Escape mid-press finds the gesture already cancelled and does nothing at all, not even a repeat of the selection-changed event, and the cancelled gesture keeps the press until its button comes up. `OnPointerCancelled` cancels and ends the press in one go, since the release will never arrive. A `Board` swap in `OnParametersSet` cancels without restoring. The board guard is `CommandHistory.Lock`/`Unlock`, held for the life of the press so every `Do`, `Undo` and `Redo` is a no-op meanwhile, plus a `PressOwnsBoard` early return on Delete, Group, Ungroup, Undo, Redo, Space, Enter, Alt+Arrow resize and the arrow nudge. The two host-driven writes guard the same way: `ClickToAdd` and a palette drop return before placing, because a refused add followed by the usual select-and-focus would have selected an instance that was never placed. Zoom, Escape, Ctrl+Tab and the snap toggle are untouched.

The old interaction layer still owns every primary press that is not on bare canvas. The listener leaves those presses alone entirely, so their compatibility mouse events fire and `ComponentContainer`'s handlers, the port and endpoint drags, the group resize handles and the edge clicks work as before. The ticket lists five canvas-level bindings to delete and three went (`@onmousedown`, `@onclick`, `@oncontextmenu`); `@onmousemove` and `@onmouseup` stay, because the connector drag over empty canvas, the group resize and the group move have no other way to see their moves and releases until tickets 74 to 76 take them onto the spine, and deleting them here would have broken three working gestures with nothing in their place. One of those gestures also lost its entry point when the canvas's `@onmousedown` went: the multi-selection's move used to start from a mousedown on empty canvas that happened to fall inside the selection's bounds. ADR 0017 makes `.selection-bounding-box` a solid hit target (`pointer-events: auto`, role `selection-bounds`), and its own `@onmousedown` now starts that move through `StartGroupMoveFromSelectionBox`. The accepted cost the decision names is real and a test pins the shape: a press on a member instance inside a multi-selection's box now lands on the box, so collapsing to that member or shift-toggling it out by pointer waits for ticket 76's release table. Role markers went on every participant, written as the `HitRole` constants in the markup so the vocabulary has one source: the container root (`instance` with its entity id, passed as a new `EntityId` parameter), port strips, ports, resize handles, edges, floating endpoints, edge labels, the LOD placeholder, the selection box and its handles.

Tests. `PanGestureTests`, `MarqueeSelectGestureTests` and `PressToKindTests` drive the gesture objects over a `FakeGestureContext`, with `PointerEvents` building the interop records for them and for the canvas-level tests alike. `DiagramCanvasPointerGestureTests` drives the rendered canvas through the four entry points with a `CanvasPointer` helper: a theory over `Enum.GetNames<GestureKind>()` with a release-reliability case, an Escape cancel case and a blur cancel case per member, each switching on the kind and throwing for one it does not know; the press-to-kind table for the `canvas` role over all three buttons with and without Shift; the fast click, the middle click, the press-anchored pan, the dropped second button, the keyboard guard, the refused palette click, the board swap, the `tabindex`, the Ctrl+Tab anchor reset and the no-focus-on-selection check. Every bUnit test that used to dispatch mouse events at the canvas now goes through the helper, the context menu tests right-click the entity itself (a right-click on empty canvas now clears the selection and opens nothing, which has its own test), and the group move tests press the selection box. `HistoryTests` covers the lock. The gesture theory is enumerated by name because the enum is internal and a public theory cannot take it as a parameter. The listener itself has no automated coverage yet; the bUnit tests prove the C# half, and the browser half is what ticket 72's interaction probe fixture exists for.

The glossary entries the checklist names describe the whole design across the tickets that build it, so they already mention members and rules this ticket does not ship (the full set of eight, the macOS Ctrl+click rule, re-sent moves, board-space pan anchoring). Nothing shipped contradicts them, and the one rule that bound this ticket's shape, that nothing at the canvas level observes which gesture is live, held: two internal properties exposing the gesture's kind and phase were written and then removed when the `Release-reliability case` entry's own warning was reread, and the theories assert behaviour instead. Four Playwright tests that drew a marquee with Shift held now draw it with a plain drag, which is the same visual state under the new mapping.

The visual suite needed two changes of its own before it could hold the new markup. The entity marker puts each entity's id into the DOM, and a demo page seeds fresh ids on every load, so every HTML snapshot of a board would have differed on every run; `ModuleInitializer` now turns on Verify's inline GUID scrubbing, and the snapshots hold `Guid_1`, `Guid_2` and so on in order of first appearance. The second run also showed Blazor's `_bl_N` element-reference stamps swapping between the two canvases of the themed-defaults page from one run to the next, now that each canvas captures one more reference and makes one more interop call on first render; a scrubber drops those attributes from the snapshot, since they are Blazor's own plumbing and say nothing about the rendering. One Playwright test changed what it clicks: the two-instance right-click test right-clicked the topmost instance, which the selection box now covers, so Playwright refused the click as intercepted and the test failed before it reached its baseline. It now right-clicks the selection box, which is where a real press inside a multi-selection lands, and the menu opens with the selection preserved.

Baseline movement, over three runs (the first found the intercepted click, the second the two scrubbing problems, the third was clean apart from the planned churn): every one of the 51 `.verified.html` files with a canvas moved, by the canvas `tabindex`, the `.diagram-canvas:focus` and `pointer-events: auto` rules in the shared style block, the role, entity and part markers on containers, ports, port strips, resize handles, edges, endpoints, labels, placeholders, the selection box and its handles, and the two scrubbers; the two palette-only pages did not move. With ids and the dropped stamps normalised away, the third run's output differed from the folded files in nothing but the re-targeted right-click test. Eight PNGs moved and were inspected pixel by pixel; the eighth is that right-click test, whose menu now opens at the selection box's centre. The four mid-marquee screenshots differ only along the band's dashed edge, because the band's top went from 204.4 to 205 pixels: the listener measures the press against the container's rect at the event where the old handler subtracted a rect fetched over interop, and the dash pattern shifted with it. The two right-click screenshots show the selected instance's selection outline, hidden before only because focus-follows-selection had left DOM focus on the instance, and now visible because the press transfers focus to the canvas; the menu itself also sits a sub-pixel lower for the same reason as the band. The custom-port screenshot shows the rectangle still selected after the connector drag, which it should be: the old canvas click handler used to fire on the drag's trailing native click and clear the selection as a side effect, and that handler is gone. Fourteen received PNGs whose only difference was the five-pixel corner noise ticket 69 describes were not folded. The run's console summary was lost again, this time to the pipeline reading it stopping at its own line limit while the container ran on; the received files are what was folded.

Not done here: `pointerType`-specific thresholds, the macOS Ctrl+click rule (ADR 0024), the `Hit stack` and the pressed-entity release table for `selection-bounds` (ticket 76 and ADR 0046), the empty-canvas menu ADR 0022's table names (ticket 10, since the existing menu has nothing to offer with no selection), a commit path on the gesture context (the first gesture that commits brings it), and the `Menu verdict` marker for author content.
