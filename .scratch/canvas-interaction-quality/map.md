# Canvas Interaction Quality

Wayfinder map.

## Destination

A written spec (PRD) for a full interaction-quality pass on the D12Canvas canvas, backed by amended or superseded interaction-layer ADRs, and carried through to implementation tickets in this same effort. The seed was a list of ten felt defects and gaps from real use of `D12Canvas.App` — each now restated in the ticket that owns it — but the destination is deliberately wider than that list: the canvas should feel like a first-class direct-manipulation surface, not a component library that technically supports the gestures. Mouse and trackpad are first-class; touch and pen are not built, but must not be foreclosed.

## Notes

- Domain: a Blazor DOM/CSS diagramming canvas. Consult `domain-modeling` when naming what this effort introduces — there is currently no term for "the gesture that owns a pointer press", and `CONTEXT.md` will need one. Consult `codebase-design` when shaping the arbitration layer; it is a deep-module candidate replacing logic currently smeared across `stopPropagation` directives and nine `_isXxx` flags.
- **Reopenable:** ADRs 0004 (persistence format), 0005 (connector/edge model), 0006 (selection model), 0008 (property panel and layering), 0009 (tool modes and interaction model), 0010 (keyboard-accessible interaction). Supersede rather than accumulate contradictory amendments where a decision genuinely changes.
- **ADR 0004 was moved from settled to reopenable while resolving ticket 09**, and only for the asset-storage seam ([Asset storage seam](issues/23-asset-storage-seam.md)) — pasted image bytes are the first content the envelope has no good home for. Nothing else about the format is reopened by this; the two-phase `Props` round-trip, the versioned envelope and the host-owns-storage boundary all still hold.
- **Settled, not open for re-litigation:** ADRs 0001 (registration contract), 0002 (chrome vs board content), 0003 (board state model), 0011 (unbounded zoom/LOD/grid), 0012 (chrome theming contract). Work within them.
- **Non-foreclosure constraint** — binds on the arbitration model ticket: the model must be expressible over pointer/touch input later without redesign. Touch is not being built; it is being kept cheap to add.
- Deliverable spans three projects: `D12Canvas` is the deliverable itself, `D12Canvas.Demo` gains pages so the Playwright suite can cover new visual states, and `D12Canvas.App` gets wired as the acceptance surface (its `BoardEditor` currently mounts neither `PropertyPanel` nor any of this effort's new chrome).
- Standing rule from `AGENTS.md` applies throughout and bites hard here: any change touching rendered markup or a shared `<style>` block requires a full Playwright visual-test run in the pinned Docker image with `-parallel none` before commit. Nearly every ticket in this map touches markup.
- Interaction quality resists paper specification. Prefer `prototype` tickets over `grilling` tickets wherever the real question is "how does this feel" rather than "what are the rules".
- **tldraw is a design reference only, never a source of code.** Its `LICENSE.md` requires a commercial licence key and forbids production use without one. Ticket 03's teardown cites its constants and structure as evidence; nothing may be lifted. Excalidraw (MIT) carries no such restriction. Both repositories move fast on exactly the subjects that teardown covers, so it pins tldraw at `ef6e81c` and Excalidraw at `4872083c` — re-verify rather than assume when following a citation.

## Decisions so far

<!-- one line per closed ticket: enough to judge relevance, then open the link for the detail -->

- [What the browser actually delivers for trackpad input](issues/02-trackpad-input-research.md) — the pinch → `wheel` + synthesised `ctrlKey` convention is real in all three engines, but nothing distinguishes it from a genuine Ctrl+scroll and nothing needs to: both mean "zoom about the cursor", and deriving zoom multiplicatively from `deltaY` (`exp(-deltaY/100)`, Chromium's own inverse) rather than a fixed ±0.1 step handles both device classes in one path. No "gesture ended" signal exists in any engine — a short idle timeout is the portable answer, and it matters for undo granularity, not smoothness. Headline: **`@onwheel:preventDefault` is a silent no-op on the pinned .NET 9 runtime** (Blazor delegates to a passive-by-default `document` listener; the fix landed in 10.0-preview7 and was not backported), so the wheel listener moves into `DiagramCanvas.razor.js` as a non-passive listener on the container — which is the better design regardless of runtime. Pointer Events cannot see a trackpad gesture at all; `gesturestart` would double-count on Safari. Surfaced [Wheel-driven pan and zoom model](issues/17-wheel-pan-and-zoom-model.md).

- [Interaction teardown of reference tools](issues/03-reference-tool-interaction-teardown.md) — Miro, FigJam, tldraw (`ef6e81c`) and Excalidraw (`4872083c`) read against their own sources, organised by gesture. All four classify a press **once, at press, against a hit target**, and separate "pointing at X" from "doing X" with a screen-pixel movement threshold D12Canvas lacks entirely. Confirms ADR 0006's marquee/shift-click, ADR 0008's layering, ADR 0010's tab stops, ADR 0009's one-shot placement — and confirms ADR 0007's one-entry-per-gesture *structurally* via tldraw's mark-based history with no ephemeral write path, which de-risks ticket 05's hardest constraint. Contradicts ADR 0005 hardest: **no reference tool exposes discrete ports as the primary attachment model**, all bind to a normalized anchor with named sides as sugar, and `EdgeEndpoint` cannot express the auto/centre case that matters most. Also finds ADR 0009's "Select is the only persistent state" too strong (held-vs-entered-vs-one-shot is the right restatement) and its empty-canvas no-menu premise now false. Surfaced [Hit-test participation and hit-region geometry](issues/18-hit-test-participation.md), [Edge attachment without a named port](issues/19-edge-attachment-without-named-port.md), [Drag past the viewport edge](issues/20-drag-past-viewport-edge.md), [Create-adjacent-and-connect](issues/21-create-adjacent-and-connect.md).

- [Gesture release reliability: enumerate the leak paths](issues/04-gesture-release-reliability.md) — reproduced in a real browser (harness on `research/gesture-leak-probe`): **all six canvas gestures leak**, and the headline path needs no unusual release location at all. `HandleMouseDown` awaits a `getContainerDimensions` round-trip *before* setting `_isPanning`, so a fast enough click has its `mouseup` processed first and the flag is set on a gesture already over — an ordinary click on empty canvas leaves the canvas panning, invisibly, until the pointer next moves. That one is unreachable by static reading; it is an ordering hazard, not a missing listener. The rest follow from every gesture terminating solely via an `@onmouseup` on one element: release outside `.diagram-canvas` leaks pan/group-move/group-resize/connector; release over any `ComponentContainer` leaks the marquee *entirely inside the canvas*, whose `@onmouseup:stopPropagation` swallows it; and a dragged instance released past the `overflow: hidden` clip edge both leaks and never fires `OnMoved`, so the move never enters history — Ctrl+Z afterwards deleted the instance instead. A leaked connector drag then consumes the next click as its drop point, silently writing an edge. Ruled out: the 197px strip below `.diagram-canvas` is *not* dead (`.canvas-content` overflows into it and still hit-tests). Escape clears only the connector drag; a leaked marquee hijacks the next plain drag before any recovery. **No existing Playwright test could ever have caught any of this** — every drag test releases inside the starting element and none moves the pointer afterwards, which ticket 15 now has as an explicit test shape. Also found the standard ports and edge resize handles are exactly concentric with the handles winning on paint order, so a port is unpressable at its own centre (evidence for [Hit-test participation and hit-region geometry](issues/18-hit-test-participation.md) and [Port affordance model](issues/06-port-affordance-model.md)). Surfaced [Gesture cancellation and revert semantics](issues/22-gesture-cancellation-semantics.md).

- [Clipboard and duplication model](issues/09-clipboard-and-duplication-model.md) — **the system clipboard is the only clipboard** (no internal one, so nothing can go stale), carrying ADR 0004's board envelope *verbatim* in `text/plain`, recognised structurally — no new format, no second serializer, and pasting a saved board's text merges it in. Access is via the DOM `copy`/`cut`/`paste` events, since the `paste` event is the only permission-free read path in any engine, so **`Ctrl+C`/`X`/`V` are not keydown rows at all**; only `Ctrl+D`/`Ctrl+A` are. What travels is the selection closed over **interior edges** (both endpoints inside the set), groups recursively, ids always regenerated across five references — the missable one being `PortDef.Id`, since `CustomPortEndpoint` names the port's own GUID. Paste lands on the **paste anchor** (pointer, viewport centre otherwise), translating as a rigid body, cascading `+20,+20` only on an unchanged anchor. Duplicate never touches the clipboard — copy-then-paste would clobber the user's real clipboard. Select-all takes top-level entities only, and **edges cannot join a multi-selection**, so select-all-then-delete does not clear a board. No new command type — paste/duplicate/cut are `CompositeCommand`s of existing primitives. Exposed one trap: cut on a lone edge must be a no-op, not a delete, since copy no-ops there. Scope change: deciding where pasted image *bytes* live would amend ADR 0004, so **0004 moved from settled to reopenable** and the seam became its own ticket. Surfaced [Asset storage seam](issues/23-asset-storage-seam.md).

## Not yet specified

- **Implementation tickets for every decision below.** This map follows `d12canvas-next`'s shape: design tickets resolve the fog and seed ADRs, a `spec.md` is written, then implementation tickets land in this same `issues/` directory. None can be phrased until the decisions they implement exist.
- **Cursor and micro-feedback vocabulary.** What cursor appears over each affordance, whether a drag shows a ghost or moves the real element, whether a dimension readout appears mid-resize. Blocked on the arbitration model and the port affordance model, since both change what affordances exist.
- **Render cost under live gesture geometry.** Tickets 01/02 of `d12canvas-next` established that Blazor re-rendering every mounted child was the real bottleneck, fixed via skip-if-unchanged. Making edges follow a drag live reintroduces per-mousemove render pressure on a different path. Whether that needs its own budget, or falls out of the live-geometry decision, is not yet clear.
- **Whether `D12Canvas.App`'s board editor needs a chrome layout rework.** A selection-anchored property bar, a minimap and a richer context menu may not compose with the current fixed 220px palette rail plus header. Can't be phrased until those three land.
- **Multi-select refinements.** Click-through into a `Group` to select one member — ADR 0006 explicitly punted this to implementation time. The "select all" semantics that used to sit here are decided (top-level entities only, ADR 0013); this is what remains of the patch.
- **Edges cannot participate in a multi-selection.** `_selectedEdgeId` is a single exclusive slot, so an edge is never mixed into `_selectedInstanceIds` — which means select-all excludes edges, select-all-then-delete leaves every edge behind, and no gesture can act on several edges at once. Ticket 09 hit this and deliberately declined to fix it, since widening the slot amends ADR 0006's selection model. Blocked on the arbitration model (01) and hit-test participation (18), which own the seams it would land on. `OnDeletePressed` already carries a neighbouring unresolved case — a `Group` left referencing deleted members — that probably belongs with it.
- **Remembered-offset duplicate chaining.** ADR 0013 took the flat `+20,+20` offset; the reference bar's better behaviour is tldraw's, which remembers the last Alt-drag clone's offset in `duplicateProps` and replays it on each `Ctrl+D`, chaining so a third press continues the run and invalidating on selection change — Figma does the same and repeats rotation too. The teardown names the principle worth stealing: *you can get the ergonomics of a mode by remembering a gesture's parameters, without entering one.* Needs a reliable "a move gesture just committed, by this delta" signal, which ticket 04 proved does not currently exist (a drag released past the clip edge never fires `OnMoved`). Blocked on the arbitration model (01) and live gesture geometry (05).
- **Latched-versus-live modifier semantics.** tldraw's Alt-clone toggles mid-drag (rewinding history to the mark so undo stays clean); Excalidraw deliberately latches `withCmdOrCtrl` at press, in its own words, "otherwise it would have weird results (stuff jumping all over the screen)". Whichever way the arbitration model goes, this needs to be a per-modifier decision with a stated reason — but it cannot be phrased before that model exists.
- **Element locking as a board concept.** Documented with the same precise semantic in all three reference tools that have it: removed from pointer hit-testing, still reachable by keyboard and panel. Blocked on the hit-test participation ticket, which decides whether that seam exists at all.
- **Whether chrome should hide while a *property* is being judged, not only while a gesture runs.** tldraw's `isChangingStyle` is a self-expiring one-second flag suppressing all three canvas overlays on a style change, a keyboard nudge or a paste. Cheap and clearly right, but meaningless until ticket 08's property bar exists.
- **Bidirectional numeric property fields.** Excalidraw's Stats panel fields are simultaneously typeable inputs and drag-to-scrub controls with a declared sensitivity — a third option between ADR 0008's docked panel and ticket 08's floating bar that neither currently contemplates.
- **Visual-clamped, hit-unclamped affordance sizing at extreme zoom-out.** Pure screen-constant sizing makes handles swamp the content at 0.05×; tldraw clamps the *drawn* circle at a 0.25 zoom divisor while leaving the invisible target full size. A refinement of ticket 06 rather than a separate question, but sharper than that ticket's "hit-target sizing under zoom" currently states.

## Out of scope

- **Rotation, and any change giving `Bounds` an angle.** Ruled out while naming the destination: rotation propagates into hit-testing, port placement, edge geometry and the persistence format, which would reopen ADRs 0003 and 0004 — both of which this effort holds settled. Aspect-lock and proportional multi-resize were bundled with it and go out with it; either may return as a fresh effort.
- **Touch and pen as built, tested features.** Non-foreclosed by the arbitration model, but not delivered here — there is no device-testing story in this repo and standing one up is beyond this destination.
- **Real-time multi-user collaboration.** Inherited from `d12canvas-next`; unchanged. Ticket 03 catalogued the presence and facilitation affordances this covers — cursor chat, spotlight/follow, stamps, timers, voting, comments — recorded there for completeness rather than to reopen it.
- **Containers with real parenting** — Miro frames and containers, FigJam sections, Excalidraw frames. Ruled out on ticket 03's evidence: all four reference tools have them and they are genuinely load-bearing for organising a large board, but membership is a parent/child relationship, and FigJam's own accessibility docs say it outright — "Objects placed inside a section are considered children of that section." That reopens ADR 0003's flat, ownership-tree-free `Board`, which this map holds settled. A fresh effort.
- **Named layers** — Miro's Layers panel with an active layer. Same parenting reason as containers, plus ADR 0008 settled layering as `ZIndex` arithmetic with no grouping-aware stacking.
- **Auto-layout of a connected structure** — Miro's mind map with auto-layout on by default, "Layout nodes", tidy-up into a grid. Ticket 12 covers explicit align and distribute on a selection, which is the tractable part; automatic re-layout of a graph is a problem with its own literature and no shared boundary with interaction quality.
- **Changing the rendering strategy for ephemeral chrome.** tldraw's SDK 5.0 replaced per-affordance React/SVG elements with a single 2D-canvas overlay and said so plainly — a real signal for a DOM-based canvas at scale. But D12Canvas is a Blazor DOM/CSS canvas by definition (settled in `d12canvas-next`), and this effort is about how it feels, not what it paints with. Worth remembering when the performance ceiling next comes up.
