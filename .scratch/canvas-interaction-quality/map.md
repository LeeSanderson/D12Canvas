# Canvas Interaction Quality

Wayfinder map.

## Destination

A written spec (PRD) for a full interaction-quality pass on the D12Canvas canvas, backed by amended or superseded interaction-layer ADRs, and carried through to implementation tickets in this same effort. The seed was a list of ten felt defects and gaps from real use of `D12Canvas.App` — each now restated in the ticket that owns it — but the destination is deliberately wider than that list: the canvas should feel like a first-class direct-manipulation surface, not a component library that technically supports the gestures. Mouse and trackpad are first-class; touch and pen are not built, but must not be foreclosed.

## Notes

- Domain: a Blazor DOM/CSS diagramming canvas. Consult `domain-modeling` when naming what this effort introduces, and `codebase-design` when shaping anything structural. The term this Note used to say was missing now exists: **`Pointer gesture`** (ADR 0018), deliberately one word from `CONTEXT.md`'s existing `Gesture` — the history unit — because the relationship between them is load-bearing and four pointer gestures never produce a history entry at all. The arbitration layer itself is decided: polymorphic gesture objects over an explicit context, all owned by `DiagramCanvas`, with `ComponentContainer` holding no gesture state. Note for anyone reading the older tickets: the smear was **four** mechanisms, not three — a parallel `click`/`dblclick` dispatch layer sits alongside the `stopPropagation` directives, the bubble ordering and the nine flags, refereed by `_dragMoved`.
- **Reopenable:** ADRs 0004 (persistence format), 0005 (connector/edge model), 0006 (selection model), 0008 (property panel and layering), 0009 (tool modes and interaction model), 0010 (keyboard-accessible interaction). Supersede rather than accumulate contradictory amendments where a decision genuinely changes.
- **ADR 0004 was moved from settled to reopenable while resolving ticket 09**, and only for the asset-storage seam ([Asset storage seam](issues/23-asset-storage-seam.md)) — pasted image bytes are the first content the envelope has no good home for. Nothing else about the format is reopened by this; the two-phase `Props` round-trip, the versioned envelope and the host-owns-storage boundary all still hold.
- **Settled, not open for re-litigation:** ADRs 0001 (registration contract), 0002 (chrome vs board content), 0003 (board state model), 0011 (unbounded zoom/LOD/grid), 0012 (chrome theming contract). Work within them.
- **ADR 0011 stays settled, and ADR 0017 did not reopen it** — despite making the `LOD placeholder` hittable. 0011's decision names the cost driver as *mounted interactive components* and describes the swap as replacing *the full interactive Razor component tree*; it never says the placeholder cannot be hit. The word "non-interactive" was `CONTEXT.md`'s own, and it has been corrected there. Read the ADR rather than the term before assuming a settled decision blocks something — this is the second time on this map (ticket 14 found the same about ADR 0012) that the summary was stricter than the decision.
- **Non-foreclosure constraint** — binds on the arbitration model ticket: the model must be expressible over pointer/touch input later without redesign. Touch is not being built; it is being kept cheap to add.
- Deliverable spans three projects: `D12Canvas` is the deliverable itself, `D12Canvas.Demo` gains pages so the Playwright suite can cover new visual states, and `D12Canvas.App` gets wired as the acceptance surface (its `BoardEditor` currently mounts neither `PropertyPanel` nor any of this effort's new chrome).
- Standing rule from `AGENTS.md` applies throughout and bites hard here: any change touching rendered markup or a shared `<style>` block requires a full Playwright visual-test run in the pinned Docker image with `-parallel none` before commit. Nearly every ticket in this map touches markup.
- Interaction quality resists paper specification. Prefer `prototype` tickets over `grilling` tickets wherever the real question is "how does this feel" rather than "what are the rules".
- **tldraw is a design reference only, never a source of code.** Its `LICENSE.md` requires a commercial licence key and forbids production use without one. Ticket 03's teardown cites its constants and structure as evidence; nothing may be lifted. Excalidraw (MIT) carries no such restriction. Both repositories move fast on exactly the subjects that teardown covers, so it pins tldraw at `ef6e81c` and Excalidraw at `4872083c` — re-verify rather than assume when following a citation.

## Decisions so far

- [What the browser actually delivers for trackpad input](issues/02-trackpad-input-research.md) — the pinch → `wheel` + synthesised `ctrlKey` convention is real in all three engines, but nothing distinguishes it from a genuine Ctrl+scroll and nothing needs to: both mean "zoom about the cursor", and deriving zoom multiplicatively from `deltaY` (`exp(-deltaY/100)`, Chromium's own inverse) rather than a fixed ±0.1 step handles both device classes in one path. No "gesture ended" signal exists in any engine — a short idle timeout is the portable answer, and it matters for undo granularity, not smoothness. Headline: **`@onwheel:preventDefault` is a silent no-op on the pinned .NET 9 runtime** (Blazor delegates to a passive-by-default `document` listener; the fix landed in 10.0-preview7 and was not backported), so the wheel listener moves into `DiagramCanvas.razor.js` as a non-passive listener on the container — which is the better design regardless of runtime. Pointer Events cannot see a trackpad gesture at all; `gesturestart` would double-count on Safari. Surfaced [Wheel-driven pan and zoom model](issues/17-wheel-pan-and-zoom-model.md).

- [Interaction teardown of reference tools](issues/03-reference-tool-interaction-teardown.md) — Miro, FigJam, tldraw (`ef6e81c`) and Excalidraw (`4872083c`) read against their own sources, organised by gesture. All four classify a press **once, at press, against a hit target**, and separate "pointing at X" from "doing X" with a screen-pixel movement threshold D12Canvas lacks entirely. Confirms ADR 0006's marquee/shift-click, ADR 0008's layering, ADR 0010's tab stops, ADR 0009's one-shot placement — and confirms ADR 0007's one-entry-per-gesture *structurally* via tldraw's mark-based history with no ephemeral write path, which de-risks ticket 05's hardest constraint. Contradicts ADR 0005 hardest: **no reference tool exposes discrete ports as the primary attachment model**, all bind to a normalized anchor with named sides as sugar, and `EdgeEndpoint` cannot express the auto/centre case that matters most. Also finds ADR 0009's "Select is the only persistent state" too strong (held-vs-entered-vs-one-shot is the right restatement) and its empty-canvas no-menu premise now false. Surfaced [Hit-test participation and hit-region geometry](issues/18-hit-test-participation.md), [Edge attachment without a named port](issues/19-edge-attachment-without-named-port.md), [Drag past the viewport edge](issues/20-drag-past-viewport-edge.md), [Create-adjacent-and-connect](issues/21-create-adjacent-and-connect.md).

- [Gesture release reliability: enumerate the leak paths](issues/04-gesture-release-reliability.md) — reproduced in a real browser (harness on `research/gesture-leak-probe`): **all six canvas gestures leak**, and the headline path needs no unusual release location at all. `HandleMouseDown` awaits a `getContainerDimensions` round-trip *before* setting `_isPanning`, so a fast enough click has its `mouseup` processed first and the flag is set on a gesture already over — an ordinary click on empty canvas leaves the canvas panning, invisibly, until the pointer next moves. That one is unreachable by static reading; it is an ordering hazard, not a missing listener. The rest follow from every gesture terminating solely via an `@onmouseup` on one element: release outside `.diagram-canvas` leaks pan/group-move/group-resize/connector; release over any `ComponentContainer` leaks the marquee *entirely inside the canvas*, whose `@onmouseup:stopPropagation` swallows it; and a dragged instance released past the `overflow: hidden` clip edge both leaks and never fires `OnMoved`, so the move never enters history — Ctrl+Z afterwards deleted the instance instead. A leaked connector drag then consumes the next click as its drop point, silently writing an edge. Ruled out: the 197px strip below `.diagram-canvas` is *not* dead (`.canvas-content` overflows into it and still hit-tests). Escape clears only the connector drag; a leaked marquee hijacks the next plain drag before any recovery. **No existing Playwright test could ever have caught any of this** — every drag test releases inside the starting element and none moves the pointer afterwards, which ticket 15 now has as an explicit test shape. Also found the standard ports and edge resize handles are exactly concentric with the handles winning on paint order, so a port is unpressable at its own centre (evidence for [Hit-test participation and hit-region geometry](issues/18-hit-test-participation.md) and [Port affordance model](issues/06-port-affordance-model.md)). Surfaced [Gesture cancellation and revert semantics](issues/22-gesture-cancellation-semantics.md).

- [Clipboard and duplication model](issues/09-clipboard-and-duplication-model.md) — **the system clipboard is the only clipboard** (no internal one, so nothing can go stale), carrying ADR 0004's board envelope *verbatim* in `text/plain`, recognised structurally — no new format, no second serializer, and pasting a saved board's text merges it in. Access is via the DOM `copy`/`cut`/`paste` events, since the `paste` event is the only permission-free read path in any engine, so **`Ctrl+C`/`X`/`V` are not keydown rows at all**; only `Ctrl+D`/`Ctrl+A` are. What travels is the selection closed over **interior edges** (both endpoints inside the set), groups recursively, ids always regenerated across five references — the missable one being `PortDef.Id`, since `CustomPortEndpoint` names the port's own GUID. Paste lands on the **paste anchor** (pointer, viewport centre otherwise), translating as a rigid body, cascading `+20,+20` only on an unchanged anchor. Duplicate never touches the clipboard — copy-then-paste would clobber the user's real clipboard. Select-all takes top-level entities only, and **edges cannot join a multi-selection**, so select-all-then-delete does not clear a board. No new command type — paste/duplicate/cut are `CompositeCommand`s of existing primitives. Exposed one trap: cut on a lone edge must be a no-op, not a delete, since copy no-ops there. Scope change: deciding where pasted image *bytes* live would amend ADR 0004, so **0004 moved from settled to reopenable** and the seam became its own ticket. Surfaced [Asset storage seam](issues/23-asset-storage-seam.md).

- [Align and distribute commands](issues/12-align-and-distribute-commands.md) — eight actions (six aligns, distribute per axis) computed against the **selection bounds**, with no key object: `_selectedInstanceIds` is a `HashSet`, so "first selected" is not merely unused but unrecoverable, and marquee would fill any such order in hit-test sequence anyway. The finding most likely to be mis-implemented: **`ExpandedSelection()` is the wrong tool here** — every existing multi-entity operation flattens a `Group` to its members, which is invisible for a move (one delta for everything) and destructive for align (a delta *per entity*), so these read the **unexpanded** selection and translate a group as a **rigid body**. Distribute means **equal gaps**, ordered by centre, negative gaps allowed. Thresholds 2 and 3, counted unexpanded, hidden below rather than greyed; a zero-delta align pushes no history entry. **Snap is observed** — reversing the initial recommendation to ignore it — and the move that makes it work is snapping the **target coordinate** rather than the result: snapping each entity's `Bounds` afterwards rounds X, which survives align-left but pulls align-right and align-centre apart. Distribute rounds the *gap* (clamped to one step so it never degenerates into *pack*) and therefore **pins the first entity and lets the last drift**; both extremes stay put only with snap off. `SnapBounds` is consequently not reusable — a scalar coordinate snap is needed. No new command type; eight thin public methods over two private implementations, mirroring the z-order quartet. Recorded as **ADR 0014**. Surfaces are the context menu and keyboard, but this ticket delivers **only the commands**, which leaves it depending on neither [Context menu enrichment](issues/10-context-menu-enrichment.md) nor [Keyboard parity for new gestures](issues/16-keyboard-parity-for-new-gestures.md) in either direction — otherwise it would have sat blocked behind ticket 07 for no reason.

- [Viewport commands and minimap](issues/13-viewport-commands-and-minimap.md) — three commands (fit, selection, 100% centre-preserving) and **no reset-view**, which is an anti-feature rather than a missing fourth: ADR 0011 removed board extent, so pan-to-origin can dump the user on empty canvas. Resolved the overlap with ticket 17 into a seam — **17 owns *how zoom changes*, 13 owns *where the viewport lands*** — leaving the undocumented `PageUp`/`PageDown` bindings there. Framing goes **inside `ZoomPanTracker`** for an artefact reason, not a tidiness one: `SetScale` and `SetPanPosition` each raise `Changed`, so a two-call fit shows every listener a frame pairing the new scale with the **old pan**. Content is components **∪ resolvable edge endpoints** — ADR 0009's palette creates edges with both endpoints floating, so a components-only extent calls that board empty — and the same scan restricted to a set is what frame-selection uses, which closes a hole that would have shipped: the exclusive `_selectedEdgeId` slot means an instances-only implementation makes frame-selection **silently do nothing on a selected edge**. Margin 0.9 fixed; **framing never zooms in past 1.0** (accepting that frame-selection can zoom you out from 4×, because a `max(1.0, current)` cap makes the destination depend on where you already were); both degenerate cases are strict no-ops, empty-selection explicitly **not** falling back to fit, since an empty selection looks identical to an off-screen one. The ticket's premise that animation needs an interpolation home is **false** — `.canvas-content` already transitions `transform`, so **the state jumps and the pixels interpolate**, no per-frame render, reduced-motion free in CSS, and pointer events suppressed in flight so a mid-flight click cannot hit what is only *about to be* under the cursor. Minimap kept **in scope** against the recommendation: plain boxes, no edges, mapping **content ∪ viewport** so lostness stays visible and clickable, with the apparent cost trap dissolving structurally — boxes in board space under one transformed wrapper, split from the rect behind a board-revision `ShouldRender`, so a pan costs one transform write. Its drag takes press/release from ticket 01 rather than a hand-rolled mouse-up, making it the first test of arbitration over **chrome**. `Shift+1`/`Shift+2`/`Shift+0` bound here (3-of-4 convention, matched on `event.code` so layout-independent), deliberately diverging from ADR 0014's defer-to-parity precedent; the commands **retire ADR 0009's empty-canvas no-menu premise**. Nothing is board state — fit is derived, so a home view has nothing to store — but the sharpest finding wasn't in the ticket: with no persisted view state a board authored away from the origin **opens onto empty canvas on every load**, so the canvas now **frames all content when a `Board` is first set**. Recorded as **ADR 0015**. Two existing-code findings handed on: the ambient 100ms transform transition means pan *eases toward* the pointer rather than tracking it (ticket 17's), and `.canvas-content` still declares a vestigial `3000×3000`. Surfaced [Viewport inset for host-placed chrome](issues/24-viewport-inset-for-host-chrome.md).

- [Edge visibility and board-content theming](issues/14-edge-visibility-and-board-content-theming.md) — the ticket's own framing was the thing that needed fixing: an edge falls on **neither** side of ADR 0012's chrome-versus-content line, which is precisely why it ended up with no token *and* no data field. The real line is **who renders the pixels** — a sticky note's yellow is untouchable because an author's component paints it; an edge has no author component, so the library may default it. ADR 0012 never said only chrome may read tokens, so nothing in it is reversed and it stays **settled** (a pointer added, not ticket 09's reopening move); the token layer instead gains its **first content-role token**, `--d12-edge`, deliberately not the `--d12-connector-preview` escape-hatch case. Colour is therefore **data with a token-resolved default**: a nullable CSS colour string joining `EdgeStyle`, so **no new command type** and undo is free, with nullability the one deliberate divergence from every other colour prop (`null` = no author opinion). Persistence is one optional envelope parameter and **no `SchemaVersion` bump** — an old board deserialises to null and renders *more* correctly than when it was saved. The mechanism is the sharpest part: both obvious routes are silently broken (a presentation attribute loses to CSS; an inline `stroke` beats `.edge-line.selected`), so the author colour rides in as an inline rebind of a **separate** `--d12-edge-override`, read as `stroke: var(--d12-edge-override, var(--d12-edge))` — the renderer never learns selection exists, and "null falls through to the token" becomes literally the cascade's own fallback. Headline trap, unreachable by reading the CSS: **`<marker>` content does not inherit the referencing element's stroke**, so per-edge colour would have shipped coloured lines with grey arrowheads; `fill="context-stroke"` fixes it and **deletes a marker def** rather than adding one, since `edge-arrow-selected` exists only to carry a different fill. Selection stays **always the accent** (ADR 0006 makes it transient UI state, hence library-painted), which is the status quo tokenised so no light baseline moves, with the 2→3 width bump kept explicitly as the non-colour cue for an author who picks the accent blue. Contrast becomes a **testable guarantee about the token pair against the backdrop** and explicitly none about an authored colour; bullet 6's "arbitrary component fill" mostly **dissolves on paint order** — edges paint beneath components and are occluded rather than crossing them. Ships **no editing surface**, which is not a gap it creates: three edge properties were already settable behind a commit point commented "No panel UI calls this yet", so colour becomes the fourth behind one missing surface, handed to [Selection-anchored property bar](issues/08-selection-anchored-property-bar.md) along with the finding that **`PropertyPanel`'s discovery path is closed to entities**, not merely missing a case. Folded in as binding implementation scope: **ticket 74 did not in fact sweep all remaining chrome** — `.selection-bounding-box`, `.drag-over-affordance` (a *third* blue) and `.floating-endpoint` are still hard-coded, and the bounding box's byte-identical accent means a host retuning `--d12-accent` **desynchronises it from the marquee** directly above it. Recorded as **ADR 0016**, with a new `Edge colour` term and a widened `Theme token` entry. Surfaced [Themed visual defaults for built-in component types](issues/25-built-in-themed-defaults.md).

- [Hit-test participation and hit-region geometry](issues/18-hit-test-participation.md) — the fork the ticket never named is decided by one fact: **Blazor's `MouseEventArgs` carries no event target**, so no rearrangement of per-element handlers can ever "classify once, at press" — the information is not there, and that omission is the whole root of ticket 01's flag soup. The press therefore resolves in **JS**, on a `pointerdown` listener on `.diagram-canvas`, walking up from `event.target` to the nearest marked element and handing C# a `(role, entityId, part)` classification. Geometry in C# was the trap: the library knows only `Bounds`, so it goes **blind inside an author's component**, and since `.container-content` is `overflow: hidden` the rect *is already* an instance's true region — it pays the price and buys nothing. Not a ranked list (a C#-side table would compete with the DOM's own order, so you click the top thing and something beneath wins), and not an element identity (ticket 17 banked that `preventDefault` must be derived **synchronously**, which is ticket 04's `_isPanning` hazard exactly). Eleven roles; `author-content` among them, which makes the synchronous `preventDefault` one line and **deletes both `ComponentContainer`'s blanket `preventDefault` and `StickyNote`'s `stopPropagation` workaround** — the built-in having been the proof that an arbitrary author's `<input>` cannot focus. Chrome is excluded *structurally* by binding to `.diagram-canvas` rather than `.diagram-container`; what ticket 13 needs from 01 is capture-and-single-release, which is separable from classification. Hit regions become **elements** (the visual painted by a non-participant, since `pointer-events: stroke` cannot be widened), sized `calc(Npx / var(--d12-scale))` off a new custom property `ContentStyle` publishes — leaving 06 the numbers and taking only the edge's own 20 screen px. The headline defect: `LodSizeThreshold = 32` makes a default sticky note a placeholder at **0.16× zoom**, and the placeholder is `pointer-events: none` **and** marquee-skipped **and** Tab-skipped, so past that zoom **the whole board is unreachable by every input path that exists** — at exactly the zoom where grabbing a cluster matters most. **ADR 0011 is not reversed by fixing it and stays settled**: its decision names the cost as *mounted interactive components*, never reachability, so the error was in `CONTEXT.md`'s own wording (ticket 14's move again). Content is never dropped, affordances drop below a floor from render and hit set **together**, entities get a margin not a floor. One participation predicate with two readers (C# decides, DOM reflects), differing deliberately in one respect — the press margin does not apply to the marquee. **Locking taken here rather than graduated**, on direction: locked means *nothing modifies this*, flag on instances and edges but derived for a `Group` (ADR 0008's precedent), no `SchemaVersion` bump (ADR 0016's), a `ChangeLockedCommand` that widens the closed command set by one, **amending ADRs 0013 and 0014** to skip locked entities rather than refuse. The rule that makes it safe and that today's code violates: **pointer participation and keyboard reachability are separate properties**, welded together right now by two hand-written checks. Precedence is *affordances beat content, content follows paint order*, declared as z-indexes in one CSS block; resize handle beats port, and instances always beat edges regardless of the `PreviousZIndex()` bug — which with a 20px hit band stops being cosmetic. Recorded as **ADR 0017**, with `Hit target`, `Hit region` and `Locked` added to `CONTEXT.md`. **Unblocks [Pointer gesture arbitration model](issues/01-pointer-gesture-arbitration-model.md)** — the map's spine, whose four blockers are now all resolved.

- [Pointer gesture arbitration model](issues/01-pointer-gesture-arbitration-model.md) — the ticket counted three interacting mechanisms; there are **four**, and finding the fourth changed the answer: a parallel `click`/`dblclick` dispatch layer (three double-click meanings kept apart by their own `stopPropagation` flags) refereed against the mousedown layer by `_dragMoved`. So the model owns click and double-click too, dispatching off a press count carried on ADR 0017's classification, which deletes every `@onclick`/`@ondblclick` binding on board content and makes **`_dragMoved` disappear rather than be ported** — "was this a click?" becomes "did the gesture release from the `pointing` phase?", which the gesture already knows. It also dissolves a probe instead of surfacing one: nothing will depend on the browser's `dblclick` surviving `preventDefault` on `pointerdown`. **Eight pointer gestures**, committed **at press** with the phase inside them rather than arbitrated at the threshold — the candidate-set alternative leaves a press-to-threshold window where *nothing owns the pointer*, so there is nothing to guarantee release of and a cancellation has no owner to ask. Two members are deliberately degenerate (`SelectEdge` has no active phase, `Native` relinquishes) so all eleven roles map totally onto the owner set with no implicit else-branch; crossing the threshold under a pointing-only owner **abandons** the gesture, as a native button does. The highest-value structural claim: **`ComponentContainer` holds zero gesture state**, because a container is a box the pointer can leave and a gesture outlives the box — which is *why* ticket 04's worst leak was worst, and ADR 0017 had already removed the reason for the split. That collapses `_isMoving`/`_isGroupMoving` and `_isResizing`/`_isGroupResizing`, which are **the same two gestures implemented twice** for n=1 and n-many, and deletes the legacy `_editMode` pair — a rival implementation kept alive by one nav-linked demo page with no visual-test coverage. Gestures are **objects, not a switch**, on an explicit context rather than a back-reference, decided on ticket 22's ground: cancellation becomes a method you cannot forget to implement, so the leaks cannot return by accretion. The finding that makes ticket 17's mirroring constraint cheap: **all four synchronous decisions derive from the role alone** (`preventDefault`, `setPointerCapture`, focus transfer, threshold), so no selection state is copied into JS and the *kind* is chosen in C# after the hop. **JS owns the threshold and does not call C# below it**, making promotion implicit and giving C# the invariant that any move it receives is a real drag; the delta measures from the **press point**, keeping `final delta = release − press` exact for the fog's duplicate chaining. Four `[JSInvokable]` methods, deliberately not `addKeyboardListener`'s 21, and one reusable listener with `classify` on or off so ADR 0015's minimap inherits release guarantees instead of becoming leak path seven. Release: `pointerup` commits, `pointercancel` reverts, **`lostpointercapture` on a live gesture reverts loudly and should never fire** — ticket 04's six leaks converted into one observable event. Capture goes on **the stable element, not the hit element**, because `ShowSelectionOverlay` re-renders away the very handle being dragged when selection changes; `Native` takes no capture, and the one gesture with no guarantee is the one with no state. Ownership keyed by `pointerId`. The one gap the model did not close for free: **`preventDefault` suppresses blur, and blur is what commits an inline edit**, so JS transfers focus explicitly on every captured press. Two target predicates, **not one shared function** — sharing it would widen `isEditableTarget` and swallow `Enter` on a focused palette entry. Author content selects its instance **additively**, answering the question ADR 0017 deferred, and is the one decision in the press path allowed to be async. Touch costs exactly two commitments (`pointerId`, `pointerType` in the mapping); the stronger no-modifier-only-gestures reading was **rejected** for quietly binding ticket 07. Recorded as **ADR 0018**, amending ADR 0009's Escape row and ADR 0017's classification payload (declared, not smuggled — the third time a summary risked reading stricter than its decision), confirming ADR 0007. **Unblocks [Live gesture geometry](issues/05-live-gesture-geometry.md), [Right-button semantics and press-to-drag](issues/07-right-button-and-press-to-drag-semantics.md), [Gesture cancellation and revert semantics](issues/22-gesture-cancellation-semantics.md).** Surfaced [What remains of `ComponentContainer`'s edit mode](issues/26-component-container-edit-mode-remnant.md), [Press-time focus transfer versus focus-follows-selection](issues/27-press-focus-transfer.md).

## Not yet specified

- **Implementation tickets for every decision below.** This map follows `d12canvas-next`'s shape: design tickets resolve the fog and seed ADRs, a `spec.md` is written, then implementation tickets land in this same `issues/` directory. None can be phrased until the decisions they implement exist.
- **Cursor and micro-feedback vocabulary.** What cursor appears over each affordance, whether a drag shows a ghost or moves the real element, whether a dimension readout appears mid-resize. The arbitration model half is resolved (ADR 0018), so the remaining blocker is the port affordance model, which still changes what affordances exist — and ADR 0018 sharpened the patch rather than shrinking it, since a `pointing` phase that has not yet promoted is exactly the state a cursor or press-and-hold affordance would express, and nothing currently marks it. ADR 0017 added a dependant with a deadline attached: a **locked** entity is silently unclickable — the press falls through to `canvas` and starts a pan, with no feedback about why — and every reference tool answers that with a lock badge on hover. Locking ships rough until this patch graduates.
- **Render cost under live gesture geometry.** Tickets 01/02 of `d12canvas-next` established that Blazor re-rendering every mounted child was the real bottleneck, fixed via skip-if-unchanged. Making edges follow a drag live reintroduces per-mousemove render pressure on a different path. Whether that needs its own budget, or falls out of the live-geometry decision, is not yet clear.
- **Whether `D12Canvas.App`'s board editor needs a chrome layout rework.** A selection-anchored property bar, a minimap and a richer context menu may not compose with the current fixed 220px palette rail plus header. The minimap half is now decided (ADR 0015) and made this patch denser rather than thinner: it adds a second host-positioned chrome component, and ticket 13 deliberately pushed **a zoom/viewport control cluster** here rather than introducing it, on the grounds that every new control is one more thing a host must place (ADR 0002) — including the tempting shortcut of siting fit/100% buttons *on* the minimap, which would turn a view into a control panel. Note this patch is now distinct from the ticket ADR 0015 surfaced: [Viewport inset for host-placed chrome](issues/24-viewport-inset-for-host-chrome.md) asks whether the canvas can *know* what occludes it, which is answerable now; this asks what the host's editor should actually look like, which still waits on the property bar and the context menu.
- **Whether the edges layer belongs in the z-order at all.** Ticket 14 found that `PreviousZIndex()` returns `min - 1` while `.edges-layer` is pinned at `z-index: 0`, so **send-to-back drops a component beneath every edge** — after which edges paint across its fill, the one case ADR 0016's contrast reasoning excludes. Today's arrangement also ties the *first* placed component with the edges layer at 0, leaving DOM order to break it. ADR 0008 settled layering as `ZIndex` arithmetic over component instances and never contemplated a non-instance layer competing in the same stacking context; ADR 0008 is reopenable, so this is legitimately askable. Not yet sharp because the answer plausibly depends on whether edges can ever join a multi-selection, which changes whether an edge is a peer of a component in the z-order or a layer beneath it. **Sharpened, not resolved, by ADR 0017**: an edge's hit region is now a 20-screen-pixel band rather than a 2-unit line, so a component sent to the back would stop being *clickable* wherever an edge crosses it, not merely be painted over. ADR 0017 fixed the hit order independently — instances always beat edges — which buys time but leaves the paint arithmetic untouched, and now means hit order and paint order deliberately disagree in exactly this one case.

- **Multi-select refinements.** Click-through into a `Group` to select one member — ADR 0006 explicitly punted this to implementation time. The "select all" semantics that used to sit here are decided (top-level entities only, ADR 0013); this is what remains of the patch. Ticket 12 added a second dependant: **aligning *within* a group** waits on the same gesture, since ADR 0014 acts on top-level entities and treats a group as one rigid body, leaving no way to address its members. ADR 0018 supplies the mechanism this was missing — a press count now rides on the classification, so a double-press or modified press has somewhere to be expressed — but not the semantics, which are ticket 07's, so this stays fog rather than graduating alongside its sibling patch.
- **Remembered-offset duplicate chaining.** ADR 0013 took the flat `+20,+20` offset; the reference bar's better behaviour is tldraw's, which remembers the last Alt-drag clone's offset in `duplicateProps` and replays it on each `Ctrl+D`, chaining so a third press continues the run and invalidating on selection change — Figma does the same and repeats rotation too. The teardown names the principle worth stealing: *you can get the ergonomics of a mode by remembering a gesture's parameters, without entering one.* Needs a reliable "a move gesture just committed, by this delta" signal, which ticket 04 proved does not currently exist (a drag released past the clip edge never fires `OnMoved`). **Half unblocked by ADR 0018**, which is what makes such a signal possible at all: commitment moves off `ComponentContainer` onto a canvas-owned gesture that commits once, at release, and the press-point-anchored delta guarantees the number it would carry is exact (`final delta = release − press`). Still blocked on live gesture geometry (05).
- **Whether chrome should hide while a *property* is being judged, not only while a gesture runs.** tldraw's `isChangingStyle` is a self-expiring one-second flag suppressing all three canvas overlays on a style change, a keyboard nudge or a paste. Cheap and clearly right, but meaningless until ticket 08's property bar exists.
- **Bidirectional numeric property fields.** Excalidraw's Stats panel fields are simultaneously typeable inputs and drag-to-scrub controls with a declared sensitivity — a third option between ADR 0008's docked panel and ticket 08's floating bar that neither currently contemplates.
- **Visual-clamped, hit-unclamped affordance sizing at extreme zoom-out.** Pure screen-constant sizing makes handles swamp the content at 0.05×; tldraw clamps the *drawn* circle at a 0.25 zoom divisor while leaving the invisible target full size. A refinement of ticket 06 rather than a separate question, but sharper than that ticket's "hit-target sizing under zoom" currently states. ADR 0017 built the half of it that is mechanism — `--d12-scale`, plus the rule that a region is an element and its visual is painted by a non-participant, which is exactly what lets the two be clamped independently — and deliberately left ticket 06 the numbers.

## Out of scope

- **Rotation, and any change giving `Bounds` an angle.** Ruled out while naming the destination: rotation propagates into hit-testing, port placement, edge geometry and the persistence format, which would reopen ADRs 0003 and 0004 — both of which this effort holds settled. Aspect-lock and proportional multi-resize were bundled with it and go out with it; either may return as a fresh effort.
- **Touch and pen as built, tested features.** Non-foreclosed by the arbitration model, but not delivered here — there is no device-testing story in this repo and standing one up is beyond this destination.
- **Real-time multi-user collaboration.** Inherited from `d12canvas-next`; unchanged. Ticket 03 catalogued the presence and facilitation affordances this covers — cursor chat, spotlight/follow, stamps, timers, voting, comments — recorded there for completeness rather than to reopen it.
- **Containers with real parenting** — Miro frames and containers, FigJam sections, Excalidraw frames. Ruled out on ticket 03's evidence: all four reference tools have them and they are genuinely load-bearing for organising a large board, but membership is a parent/child relationship, and FigJam's own accessibility docs say it outright — "Objects placed inside a section are considered children of that section." That reopens ADR 0003's flat, ownership-tree-free `Board`, which this map holds settled. A fresh effort.
- **Named layers** — Miro's Layers panel with an active layer. Same parenting reason as containers, plus ADR 0008 settled layering as `ZIndex` arithmetic with no grouping-aware stacking.
- **Auto-layout of a connected structure** — Miro's mind map with auto-layout on by default, "Layout nodes", tidy-up into a grid. Ticket 12 covers explicit align and distribute on a selection, which is the tractable part; automatic re-layout of a graph is a problem with its own literature and no shared boundary with interaction quality.
- **Changing the rendering strategy for ephemeral chrome.** tldraw's SDK 5.0 replaced per-affordance React/SVG elements with a single 2D-canvas overlay and said so plainly — a real signal for a DOM-based canvas at scale. But D12Canvas is a Blazor DOM/CSS canvas by definition (settled in `d12canvas-next`), and this effort is about how it feels, not what it paints with. Worth remembering when the performance ceiling next comes up.
